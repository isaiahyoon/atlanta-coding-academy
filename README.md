# Atlanta Coding Academy

Atlanta Coding Academy is a planned nonprofit coding academy in Atlanta, Georgia, welcoming learners of all ages. Its mission is to expand access to practical coding education for people in underserved communities and equip them to use their gifts in service of God and their neighbors.

## Mission and vision

The academy is a Christian ministry expressed through practical, creative learning. It welcomes people of all ages to seek God, experience His love, and build toward a different future through purposeful work. People who are formerly incarcerated are always welcome as students and participants, with dignity and without stigma.

Students should discover that technology is more than a career path. It can help them create useful, beautiful things that serve people and honor God. The academy's deepest hope is that its students are blessed by God and redeemed through His Son, Jesus Christ.

The academy contributes to a larger vision: Atlanta becoming the greatest place in the world to raise a family.

## Program and enrollment

### Established direction

- Enrollment is open to learners of all ages.
- In-person instruction will be part of the program.
- Scholarships will be provided based on merit and financial need measured against a poverty threshold.
- Bible study is a core part of the curriculum.
- Formerly incarcerated people are welcome without stigma.

### Still to be determined

- Schedule, location, and launch timing will be set after funding and/or grants are secured.
- Whether the program will also include remote or hybrid instruction is undecided.
- The poverty threshold, scholarship rubric, and selection criteria need to be defined.
- The application process is still being designed.
- Develop age-appropriate cohorts, instruction, and safeguarding for learners across ages.

## Learning and curriculum

Learning should connect technical fundamentals to students' interests, lived experience, and real community needs. Projects should move through the full process: identify a need, plan a solution, build it, test it, and improve it with feedback.

- Keep student work authentic to each student's life story, including interests, experiences, hardships, struggles, and pain—not generic scenarios disconnected from their lives. Students choose what personal experiences to bring into their work; disclosure is never required. Help them explore practical, creative ways to use their gifts and chosen experiences to serve God's kingdom and share hope with the world, trusting that God can be glorified in redeeming areas of suffering.
- Make Bible study a core part of the curriculum. Empower students to read Scripture independently and draw directly from the King James Version, valued for its lyrical quality and superior regal expression.
- Apply <a href="https://yoonbasketballtraining.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Yoon Basketball Training</a>'s coaching approach: establish fundamentals, tailor practice, increase challenge steadily, use feedback to guide the next step, and make room for teamwork and joy.
- Teach programming and web fundamentals, then explore frontend and backend development, databases, APIs and integrations, security and privacy, testing, debugging, performance, and deployment.
- Teach Git as a core professional skill: version history, branches, conflict resolution, code review, and collaborative workflows.
- Use AI tools as personal assistants for brainstorming, explanations, planning, coding, debugging, testing, and documentation. Students must understand and verify AI-assisted work, protect private information, and remain responsible for what they build.
- Develop strong engineering communication and collaboration: learn from senior engineers with humility and curiosity, ask thoughtful questions, and mentor junior teammates with empathy and patience. Practice code review and make thoughtful technical tradeoffs alongside coding skills.
- Build cross-functional fluency: collaborate with product managers, engineers, designers, and key stakeholders; communicate clearly, use feedback, and make thoughtful tradeoffs. Prepare students to contribute within structured, hierarchical organizations while also strengthening initiative, independent judgment, and self-directed work for those who prefer greater autonomy.
- Connect projects to useful needs in Atlanta communities and to fields students may want to enter, including healthcare, finance, churches, nonprofits, community outreach, local businesses, creative work, and sports training.
- Help students see how technology can serve people, support local creators, and produce original, beautiful work that expresses their voice and can be shared with the world.

## Atlanta: people, history, and opportunity

The academy should reflect Atlanta's entrepreneurial spirit, talent, confidence, and strong work ethic. It should also acknowledge challenges—including violence, drug dealing, substance addiction, poverty, insecurity, and conflict—without romanticizing harm or defining Atlanta and its residents by hardship. Addiction is a global challenge that also affects low-income urban communities.

The academy's story should honor the richness and diversity of Black life in Atlanta and the city's educational legacy, including <a href="https://www.gatech.edu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Georgia Tech</a>, <a href="https://www.gsu.edu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Georgia State</a>, the <a href="https://aucenter.edu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Atlanta University Center Consortium</a>, and other historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs). Its historical context includes Christian civil-rights leaders such as <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther_King_Jr." target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Martin Luther King Jr.</a> and <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lewis_(civil_rights_leader)" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">John Lewis</a>; Georgia's colonial and Civil War history; and Atlanta's growth from a railroad center into an aviation hub. Treat figures such as Robert E. Lee as historically influential, not uncomplicated moral examples, and present difficult history with accuracy and moral clarity.

Buckhead is part of Atlanta's shared story. Affirm unity while acknowledging inequities and shared responsibilities across neighborhoods. Buckhead's resources can be stewarded in service of God and the wider city through partnership and mutual dignity. Southern hospitality—warmth, welcome, and neighborliness—belongs alongside Atlanta's energy as a metropolitan hub.

Atlanta's industries create practical opportunities across law, aviation, logistics, retail and home improvement, hospitality, hotels, and travel. Recognizable local connections include <a href="https://www.ups.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">UPS</a> and <a href="https://www.homedepot.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">The Home Depot</a>. Atlanta's startup ecosystem—including incubators, accelerators, founder-support programs, coworking communities, early-stage companies, and local makers—offers potential collaborators for mentorship, workshops, and student projects. Explore possibilities through <a href="https://guide.startupatlanta.com/programs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Startup Atlanta's program directory</a>; these are prospective connections, not existing partnerships. Help students see how technology can serve these fields, open career pathways, and help creators reach people. Present principled legal work as a way to pursue justice and serve neighbors.

The academy should acknowledge father absence as a challenge for some Black youth and families without treating it as universal or defining Black households by it. Respond with dignity, supportive mentorship, and trustworthy adult relationships.

Public spaces such as <a href="https://piedmontpark.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Piedmont Park</a> and the <a href="https://beltline.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Atlanta BeltLine</a> show how shared places can connect neighborhoods and welcome people. Carry that spirit into the academy with moderation, practicality, safety, and Christian conviction. Atlanta's motto, *Resurgens* ("rising again"), can express renewal and purposeful growth without romanticizing hardship. Ground ambition in Georgia's state motto: "Wisdom, Justice, Moderation."

## Faith and student experience

The complete King James Version (KJV) is the academy's foundational Scripture. Bible study should help students engage Scripture directly and develop the confidence to read it on their own. The academy values the KJV's lyrical quality and superior regal expression.

Teach the personhood and work of the Holy Spirit. John 15:26 establishes Him as a Person: Jesus identifies Him as the Comforter and Spirit of truth, sent from the Father to testify of Christ—not an impersonal force. Jesus says: “But when the Comforter is come, whom I will send unto you from the Father, even the Spirit of truth, which proceedeth from the Father, he shall testify of me:” (<a href="https://theamazingword.org/kjv/b/john/c/15/v/26" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">John 15:26, KJV</a>).

The academy is a welcoming place for people to seek God, know they are completely loved by Him, and use their learning to glorify, honor, and worship Him. Its spiritual tone may draw on themes of God dwelling among His people, grace through Christ, welcome at the table, Christ's love surpassing worldly pleasures, intimate union with Him, and obedient worship.

## Website and brand direction

The website's Atlanta swagger is regality and God-fearing wisdom: bold, high-energy polish grounded in humility, care, and purpose. Bring the founder's Los Angeles roots into the design while keeping Atlanta at the center. External website links should open in a new tab.

### Voice and content

- Pair a warm, multilingual welcome with neighbor-to-neighbor dignity, practical care, and patient encouragement. Make room for questions and steady growth; a student's worth is greater than performance.
- Draw broadly on Atlanta trap, rap, and hip-hop energy—confidence, rhythm, grit, and local identity—without naming or imitating specific artists.
- Celebrate dance and joyful movement as healthy, creative expression and a celebration of God's creation.
- Let technology amplify, not replace, human creativity. Make room for original work, multilingual expression, and students' personal voices.
- Keep the atmosphere restorative, grounded, and hopeful. Do not publish personal grief stories, private correspondence, or unapproved personal photos or poetry.

### Visual language

- Use flowing, translucent ribbon-like forms and flame-shaped accents, with deep midnight blue, electric blue, and warm sunset light.
- Set oversized editorial serif headlines against clear sans-serif body text. Use selective italics, calm blue-gray text, pale blue backgrounds, and restrained gold details. Keep typography responsive and legible on small screens.
- Draw on <a href="https://yoonbasketballtraining.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Yoon Basketball Training</a>'s visual system: editorial serif and clear sans-serif typography, deep navy and royal blue, soft mint, warm peach, and spacious layouts. Adapt it to Atlanta Coding Academy rather than copying it.
- Make Atlanta's trees a core motif: canopy for shelter and breathing room, roots for connection and community, and new growth for students developing their gifts. Combine tree forms with the ribbon-and-flame language while preserving open space, light, and readability.
- Create original visuals of a grounded Atlanta landscape: a skyline framed by a generous tree canopy, open sky, shared green space, warm light, and a place to rest beneath a tree. Write original site copy.
