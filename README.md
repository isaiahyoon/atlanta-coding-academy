# Atlanta Coding Academy

Atlanta Coding Academy is a pre-launch vision for faith-centered coding education in Atlanta, Georgia. Classes and enrollment have not begun. Its mission is to expand access to practical coding education for people in underserved communities and equip them to use their gifts in service of God and their neighbors.

The descriptions below express the vision for the academy, not programs or services currently available.

## Mission and vision

The academy is a Christian ministry expressed through practical, creative learning. It welcomes people of all ages to seek God, experience His love, and build a different future through purposeful work. People who are formerly incarcerated are always welcome as students and participants, with dignity and without stigma.

Technology can be more than a career path. It can help us create useful, beautiful things that serve people and honor God. The academy's deepest hope is that its students are blessed by God and redeemed through His Son, Jesus Christ.

The academy contributes to a larger vision: Atlanta becoming the greatest place in the world to raise a family.

## Academy and learning experience

The vision welcomes learners across ages, including people who were formerly incarcerated, with dignity and without stigma; the first program's age range is not yet determined. In-person coding instruction and Bible study are central to the planned experience. Hands-on learning would build practical skills through projects shaped by learners' interests, lived experiences, and community needs. Scholarships based on merit and financial need are a future goal, not currently available.

Age-aware instruction, safeguarding, and accessibility help learners grow in a welcoming environment. The academy values multilingual expression and supports students with patient instruction, thoughtful feedback, and room to learn at their own pace.

## Learning and curriculum

Students connect technical fundamentals to their interests, lived experiences, and real community needs. A project can begin with a question or a need, then grow through planning, building, testing, and feedback.

- **Work that means something:** Students can draw on their interests, experiences, hardships, struggles, and pain rather than working only with generic scenarios. Sharing personal experiences is always their choice. They can explore how their gifts and experiences might serve God's kingdom and share hope, trusting that God can be glorified in redeeming suffering.
- **Scripture:** Bible study gives students room to read the King James Version for themselves, appreciating its lyrical quality and superior regal expression.
- **Patient practice:** Inspired by <a href="https://yoonbasketballtraining.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Yoon Basketball Training</a>, the approach begins with fundamentals, adapts practice to the learner, and builds challenge steadily—with feedback, teamwork, and joy along the way.
- **Technical foundations:** Programming and web fundamentals lead into frontend and backend development, databases, APIs and integrations, security and privacy, testing, debugging, performance, and deployment.
- **Git and collaboration:** Students work with version history, branches, conflict resolution, code review, and collaborative workflows.
- **Thoughtful use of AI:** AI can assist with brainstorming, explanations, planning, coding, debugging, testing, and documentation. Students remain responsible for understanding and verifying its work and protecting private information.
- **Engineering communication:** Students can learn from senior engineers with humility and curiosity, ask thoughtful questions, and mentor junior teammates with empathy and patience. Code review, feedback, and thoughtful technical tradeoffs are part of the craft.
- **Working across teams:** Collaboration with product managers, engineers, designers, and key stakeholders builds communication, feedback, and decision-making skills. Students can learn to work within structured, hierarchical organizations while also developing initiative, independent judgment, and self-directed work.
- **Atlanta in the work:** Projects can address local needs and interests across healthcare, finance, churches, nonprofits, community outreach, local businesses, creative work, and sports training.
- **Creativity in service:** Technology can support local creators and give students a way to make original, beautiful work in their own voice—and share it with the world.

## Atlanta: people, history, and opportunity

Atlanta's entrepreneurial spirit, talent, confidence, and strong work ethic give the academy much to celebrate. The city also faces real challenges—including violence, drug dealing, substance addiction, poverty, insecurity, and conflict. Those challenges deserve honest attention without romanticizing harm or defining Atlanta and its residents by hardship. Addiction is a global challenge that also affects low-income urban communities.

Atlanta's fashion culture brings its own creativity, individual style, inventive color, and confidence. Its imaginative combinations are part of the inspiration for the academy's visual identity, alongside the people who bring the culture to life.

Atlanta's story includes the richness and diversity of Black life, the Christian civil-rights leadership of <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther_King_Jr." target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Martin Luther King Jr.</a> and <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lewis_(civil_rights_leader)" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">John Lewis</a>, and an educational legacy that includes <a href="https://www.gatech.edu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Georgia Tech</a>, <a href="https://www.gsu.edu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Georgia State</a>, the <a href="https://aucenter.edu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Atlanta University Center Consortium</a>, and other historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs). Georgia's colonial and Civil War history, and Atlanta's growth from a railroad center into an aviation hub, are part of that story too. Historical influence is not the same as moral example: figures such as Robert E. Lee belong in a history told with accuracy and moral clarity.

Buckhead is part of Atlanta's shared story, alongside neighborhoods with different resources and needs. The academy believes in partnership, mutual dignity, and stewarding resources in service of God and the wider city. Southern hospitality—warmth, welcome, and neighborliness—belongs alongside Atlanta's energy as a metropolitan hub.

Atlanta's industries create practical opportunities across law, aviation, logistics, retail and home improvement, hospitality, hotels, and travel. Recognizable local connections include <a href="https://www.ups.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">UPS</a> and <a href="https://www.homedepot.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">The Home Depot</a>. Technology can serve these fields, open career pathways, and help creators reach people; principled legal work can pursue justice and serve neighbors. The city's startup ecosystem—including incubators, accelerators, founder-support programs, coworking communities, early-stage companies, and local makers—gives students a window into entrepreneurship, mentorship, workshops, and project work. <a href="https://guide.startupatlanta.com/programs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Startup Atlanta's program directory</a> highlights organizations and programs across that community.

Father absence is a challenge for some Black youth and families, though it is neither universal nor a definition of Black households. Supportive mentorship and trustworthy adult relationships can make a difference, always grounded in dignity.

Public spaces such as <a href="https://piedmontpark.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Piedmont Park</a> and the <a href="https://beltline.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Atlanta BeltLine</a> show how shared places can connect neighborhoods and welcome people. The academy carries that spirit into its work through moderation, practicality, safety, and Christian conviction. Atlanta's motto, *Resurgens* ("rising again"), speaks to renewal and purposeful growth; Georgia's state motto, "Wisdom, Justice, Moderation," keeps ambition grounded.

## Faith and student experience

The complete King James Version (KJV) is the academy's foundational Scripture. Bible study helps students engage Scripture directly and grow confident reading it on their own. The academy values the KJV's lyrical quality and superior regal expression.

John 15:26 establishes the personhood and work of the Holy Spirit: Jesus identifies Him as the Comforter and Spirit of truth, sent from the Father to testify of Christ—not an impersonal force. Jesus says: “But when the Comforter is come, whom I will send unto you from the Father, even the Spirit of truth, which proceedeth from the Father, he shall testify of me:” (<a href="https://theamazingword.org/kjv/b/john/c/15/v/26" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">John 15:26, KJV</a>).

The academy is a welcoming place for people to seek God, know they are completely loved by Him, and use their learning to glorify, honor, and worship Him. Its spiritual foundation includes God dwelling among His people, grace through Christ, welcome at the table, Christ's love surpassing worldly pleasures, intimate union with Him, and obedient worship.

## Website and brand direction

The academy's visual identity brings together Atlanta's confidence and creativity with the founder's Los Angeles roots: regal and bold, yet grounded in humility, care, and God-fearing wisdom.

The academy's website is a lightweight static site. Its <a href="./index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">home page</a> shares the mission, learning approach, and Atlanta roots; styles are in <a href="./styles.css" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">styles.css</a>. The visual identity features a <a href="./assets/burning-tree.png" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">living tree and blue flame logo</a> alongside an Atlanta hero image at <a href="./assets/atlanta-at-sunset.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">assets/atlanta-at-sunset.jpg</a>.

The home page has an English/한국어 switch; `?lang=ko` links directly to the Korean view. The translation is in <a href="./assets/ko.js">assets/ko.js</a> and requires JavaScript; without it the page remains in English. The English view quotes the KJV; the Korean view quotes complete passages from The Amazing Word's 개역한글판 1961 (KRV), attributed to 대한성서공회. The academy's envisioned Bible study remains rooted in the KJV. Social sharing previews use the static English metadata even for the Korean URL. The Korean wording is a draft for review and does not promise Korean-language instruction or support.

The site uses Umami Cloud for pageview analytics. The tracking script and public website ID are configured in `index.html`; local visits can also appear in the Umami dashboard.

The academy offers a multilingual, neighborly, and patient welcome: a student's worth is greater than performance. Atlanta trap, rap, and hip-hop contribute confidence, rhythm, grit, and local identity; dance and joyful movement celebrate creativity and God's creation. Technology can amplify human creativity without replacing it, making room for original work and individual voices. Personal grief stories, correspondence, photos, and poetry remain private unless their owners approve their sharing.

The living tree and sustained blue flame stand at the heart of the identity. Its canopy suggests shelter, its roots connection, and its new growth the gifts students are developing. Fertile midnight-navy and muted-plum earth, quiet champagne-gold seams, and aqua shoots suggest nourishment and a foundation for flourishing. The wider visual vision includes an Atlanta skyline, generous trees, open sky, shared green space, warm light, and room to rest.

Midnight navy, pearl, lavender, aqua, Crosbie pink and yellow, and champagne gold come together in gradients inspired by the supplied wallpaper studies and Atlanta fashion. Editorial serif headlines, clear sans-serif text, and spacious layouts also draw inspiration from <a href="https://yoonbasketballtraining.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Yoon Basketball Training</a>. The palette also takes cues from editions of <a href="https://theamazingword.org/about" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">The Amazing Word</a>:
- **KJV:** `#fffafa`, `#cae4f6`, `#f7d7e5`; accent `#456a83`
- **KRV (1961):** `#4b7395`, `#bed8ea`
- **Lutherbibel (1912):** `#8a6c22`, `#fff1bd`, `#b9534b`

Learning and Scripture cards reveal a soft ribbon-inspired glow and gold-edged accent on hover; Scripture links offer the same treatment on keyboard focus. Reduced-motion settings remove the card lift.
